Assuming "Prairie" is a typo for "predict," "parse," or "track," the straightforward answer is: No, the TWSE API cannot directly tell you why international investment banks made specific trades. The API provides raw quantitative transaction data—the "what," "when," and "how much"—but it does not contain the internal rationale, proprietary algorithms, or specific strategies of these institutions.

However, it is entirely feasible to ingest this API data and correlate it with external datasets to predict or deduce their trading behavior using predictive models or autonomous AI frameworks.

What the TWSE API Actually Provides

In Taiwan's financial regulatory system, international investment banks and foreign asset managers are categorized collectively as Foreign Institutional Investors (FINI), which falls under the broader "Foreign Investors" (外資及陸資) classification.

The TWSE Open Data portal provides several highly reliable, end-of-day datasets crucial for tracking this group:

Total Market Net Buy/Sell: Daily aggregate trading volume and transaction value for Foreign Investors across the entire exchange.

Individual Stock Net Buy/Sell: A precise daily breakdown of which specific tickers (e.g., TSMC, Quanta, Alchip) Foreign Investors bought or sold, and the net volume of shares accumulated or distributed.

Holdings Data: The total percentage of outstanding shares currently held by Foreign Investors for each listed company.

Critical Limitations to Consider

Firm-Level Anonymity: Due to strict privacy rules and regulatory protections, the API aggregates all foreign entities into a single category. You cannot query the API to separate Morgan Stanley's algorithmic trades from Goldman Sachs' block trades.

Lagged Reporting: The precise breakdown of institutional net buy/sell is an end-of-day report, typically published around 15:00 CST. While real-time market data feeds show trades executing intraday, they do not tag the buyer or seller's institutional identity on a tick-by-tick basis.

Lack of Causality: The data is purely transactional and retrospective.

Building a Predictive Pipeline

To deduce the "what made them buy and sell" aspect, a standard quantitative approach involves building a robust data pipeline—potentially leveraging scalable cloud databases like Supabase for storage and API gateways for data ingestion—to run AI models against the TWSE data alongside the external variables that dictate global capital flows:

Macroeconomic Indicators: TWD/USD exchange rates, US Treasury yields, and Federal Reserve policy shifts. Foreign capital allocations are highly sensitive to currency risk and shifting global liquidity.

Global Tech Correlation: Because the Taiwanese economy and equity market are heavily anchored by the semiconductor and electronics supply chain, foreign institutional behavior on the TWSE is strongly correlated with US tech indices (like the NASDAQ or SOX) and the upstream demands of global AI and computing companies.

Alternative Data: Parsing corporate material information disclosures (also accessible via TWSE APIs), tracking supply chain bottlenecks, and analyzing sentiment from earnings calls.
